Usable Compute

AI infrastructure models, standards, and decision frameworks.

An open, reproducible research program on the layers that decide how much of an AI cluster actually does useful work — and why fixing them usually beats buying more GPUs. Every result is seeded, tested, adversarially reviewed, and honest about what a skeptic should attack.

usable = nominal × network × scheduling × recovery × placement, under a governance layer

The series

Each repository isolates one factor, in the field's own metrics, with a runnable artifact.

GPU Cluster Networking

Past a scale threshold, interconnect bandwidth — not GPU count — caps AI training throughput.

GPU Cluster Scheduling paper

Allocation is where capacity is realized or lost. Three policies face an identical Meta-calibrated job stream through power contraction, node failures, and inference surges: an intent-based closed loop realizes 85.8% of the envelope where a rigid FIFO-plus-backfill configuration realizes 77.8% — ~78 GPUs' worth on a 1,024-GPU cluster, recovered without buying anything, while inference SLO attainment rises from 0.968 to 0.998. Priority tiers alone recover almost nothing. The largest single source is mundane: refusing to peak-provision inference.

Chaos Fidelity Standard standard · pip

A machine-checkable standard for which fault injection tests which layer. A pod kill is not an XID; tc/netem is not an InfiniBand flap.

Reliability Economics

Which recovery policy wins in which failure regime — a phase diagram in ETTR and dollars, validated against Meta's published reliability numbers.

Governed Autonomy pip

An autonomous control plane over clusters and networks: separated planes, a referee, an autonomy ladder earned by chaos experiments, and a sourced latency hierarchy from human intent to nanosecond in-ASIC reflexes.

Compute↔Power Placement

When does moving a job to cheaper power beat staying? Energy is ~3% of GPU-hour cost — so schedule against scarcity, don't arbitrage the spread. A fleet model prices the scheduler against itself with its ranking removed: ranking the curtailment hours by price wins in every simulated price year, and the headline saving is bound by the assumed spike ceiling — 4.8% at $5,000/MWh, 2.7% at ERCOT's $2,000 real-time cap.

Edge↔Neocloud Placement pip

Where AI workloads belong on the operator's footprint — tower, hub, metro PoP, central factory. The tower is never the recommended tier; latency is the weakest edge argument; fronthaul (~20 km), data gravity, and sovereignty do the actual forcing.

AI-RAN ↔ Neocloud Resiliency pip

The last mile priced in nines: a single-fiber edge site is a ~2.7-nines site; multi-transport bonding helps only the tenant classes; the generator — not a third link — buys the decisive nine.

Cooling & the PUE Ladder pip

Heat out, priced in PUE, megawatts, and seconds: a 120 kW rack leaves only two rungs standing; a PUE point converts to ~1.9 MW of unqueued capacity per 10 MW feed; and one adoption mechanism reproduces the survey "plateau" and hyperscale ~1.1 fleets at once.

The Span Admission Contract scale-across · pip

An admission contract for jobs that cross a hall boundary: probe → measurement → envelope → verdict, with synchronous training capped at the two tightest span classes. The verdict flips out of a measured delay, not a config knob — the same job admits at 40 km and is refused at 400. Four tenant predicates (1.1.0) refuse an organization that has spent its slice quota or two organizations on one wavelength; an envelope that declares no tenancy is admitted with the unchecked lines printed, not refused.

Optical Circuit Intent scale-across · pip

What to ask a reconfigurable optical plant for, and what it costs: vendor-neutral verbs, a retune legality ladder with an exact piecewise cost, a thermal-drift ledger, and failover that verifies the replacement circuit before tearing the old one down. Since 1.1.0, read against one measured link — the HEDGE testbed’s raw transponder and iperf logs, fetched and SHA-pinned — where the physical layer’s uncorrectable-FEC alarm ran 88 s and 39 s ahead of an outage that began inside the transponder poll in which the last wavelength failed, and every failing wavelength’s drop was closer to the 20 dB per decade branch than to the 10 dB default the drift model shipped with, and steeper than both. Since 1.2.0, bound to one documented controller interface — the TAPI 2.1.5 data tree under the TR-547 v1.2 agreement — with every call handed back rather than sent, and the one mock it has been run through pinned where it departs from the agreement, in ten places.

Slice-Packing a Torus Pod scale-across · pip

The allocation layer of a torus pod — packing, cordon, re-embedding, tenancy, reconstitution — anchored by the series’ first fleet measurement: Titan’s 30,207 GPUs (100,889 GPU-years). Ranking cohorts by hazard captures 55% of held-out deaths on a 30% budget, yet the eviction inequality says preemption pays only when a drain costs under ~3% of a rebuild: spend hazard estimates on placement, not eviction.

Tools you can install

The standards ship as commands, not just prose — run them in your own CI.

Chaos-fidelity linter — reject a wrong-layer chaos experiment:

pip install ai-cluster-chaos-fidelity
chaos-fidelity my-experiment.yaml

Autonomy promotion gate — refuse an unearned autonomy promotion:

pip install governed-autonomy-gate
promotion-gate my-record.yaml

Placement gate — block an edgeward placement no gate forces:

pip install edge-continuum-placement
edge-placement evaluate -w frontier-pretrain -t tower  # exit 1

Resilience ladder — price the next nine before buying it:

pip install airan-neocloud-resiliency
airan-resiliency ladder

Cooling density gate — block a rack no rung can cool:

pip install cooling-pue-ladder
pue-ladder gate --density 120 --rung contained-air  # exit 1

Span admission contract — refuse a cross-hall launch the facts don’t support:

pip install span-contract
spancontract validate envelope.json

Retune legality ladder — when a circuit retune is invisible, and when it stalls the job:

pip install optical-circuit-intent
ocintent ladder

Torus cordon pricer — what one chip failure costs, by where it lands:

pip install slice-packer-torus
slicepacker cordon --shape 16,16,16

How the claims are checked

Every repository ships a validation program that CI runs on each push, and every finding is filed under one of three kinds, labeled in the output: calibrated — pinned to a published figure, where passing proves the model has not drifted from its citation, not that it predicts; emergent — results the model was not tuned to produce, required to survive independent reseeding; and sanity — properties of the model's own structure, citing no evidence and claiming none.

Every registry now adds two things: it prints the anchors it does not provide, so an uncalibrated input cannot pass for a result, and its test suite breaks the model on purpose — delete a placement gate, delete the power chain, switch off the price spikes, freeze a fleet so it never modernizes — and requires named points to go red, against a control proving the unmutated registry is green.

That is not decoration. In the most recent pass it caught a generator threshold chosen after seeing the number (it failed on reseeding, and was replaced by an ordering with nothing to tune), a tie between transport options stated as a maximum rather than a mean (same fix), a latency registry that passed with three of its four gates deleted, and a concentration figure that was identical under every scheduling policy — a fact about the electricity market being reported as a result about the scheduler.

The sharpest case came from the cooling study: its held-out 2024 prediction had a band wide enough that a simulated fleet frozen at its 2014 stock — no modernization at all for a decade — cleared it on 19 of 24 random seeds. The published run happened to land on a seed where it failed, so nothing looked wrong. The band was narrowed until it is disjoint from the calibration anchor and the null model now fails on every seed. In the same pass a scheduling point that compared a pinned constant to itself was renamed to say so, and both repositories now disclose that their failure models are unconstrained by roughly an order of magnitude. Each was rewritten in public rather than quietly kept.

The method

Machine-checkable where possible; calibrated to primary sources and cross-checked against published fleet data; conditional claims with a stated "what a skeptic should attack"; and each result adversarially reviewed before release. The goal is not a slogan but a body of work an engineer can run and a reviewer can trust.

Run it on your plant

Every repository above installs with pip, exits 0, 1 or 2 on your own inputs, and re-runs its validation registry in your CI, declined list first. What the repositories cannot do alone is the step that needs your data: a span contract wired to your scheduler’s delay node, an intent bound to your controller’s own service interface points, a drift forecast read from your transponder logs rather than a laboratory’s. That work is offered as a retainer, not a product: install it, run it in your CI, and ask for it to be wired to your plant. What comes back is held to the rules on this page — every number printed by committed code, calibrated where a source exists, declined where none does — and written up so your own reviewers can attack it.
